  • Section 1: What is Distributional Semantics?
    • Core Definition & Hypothesis
      • Distributional Semantics (DS)
        • An approach that quantifies and categorizes the semantic properties of linguistic items based on their statistical distributions in large text corpora
      • The Distributional Hypothesis
        DS最重要的理论基础
        • Lexemes with similar linguistic contexts tend to have similar meanings 原理
        • Meaning is represented as a real-valued vector (word embedding) encoding these statistical distributions 表示方法
      • Interdisciplinary Nature
        • DS acts as a theoretical model (representing meaning)
        • a computational framework (acquiring meaning)
        • a practical methodology (constructing representations)
        • and a cognitive hypothesis (how language usage shapes mental concepts)
    • Theoretical Foundations (4 Historical Pillars)
      • American Structural Linguistics (Zellig Z. Harris)
        • Advocated distributionalism to make linguistics an empirical science. 由美国结构主义者倡导的分布主义，作为使语言学成为一门经验科学的核心方法
          • Environment: The total sum of neighboring linguistic contexts in which an element occurs.
          • Core Correlate: Difference of meaning correlates with difference of distribution. If two elements share identical distributions, they are functionally equivalent.
          • This idea of a correlation between meaning differences and distributional properties lies at the heart of distributional semantics
          • 帮助记忆
            • 环境决定论：语言学想成为一门真正的硬核科学，就必须讲证据。词的“环境（Environment）”就是它周围所有邻居的总和。“语义的差异，本质上就是上下文分布的差异”
            • Harris是个绝对的理性派。他说，如果“猫”和“狗”在功能上能互相替换，并且它们周围出现的邻居词极其相似，那它们在语言系统里的地位就是平等的。想研究意思？先去数它们周围的词！
      • European Structuralism (Ferdinand de Saussure)
        • Valeur (Value): A lexeme's function is strictly defined by its functional distinctiveness (differences) from other lexemes within the whole system.
          • 一个词位之所以具有价值，仅仅是因为它与其他词位不同。这种关于语言元素功能独特性差异的观点，突出了系统作为一个整体的重要性，因为差异不可能脱离系统本身而孤立存在
        • functional differences can be divided into
          • Syntagmatic Relations
            • Ordered, sequential combinations of lexemes co-occurring in a sentence (e.g., drink + water)
            • 组合关系，存在于顺序组合中共同出现的词位之间
          • Paradigmatic Relations
            • Substitution relations between lexemes that do not co-occur but share the same sequential contexts (e.g., water, tea, and juice can all substitute after drink)
            • 聚合关系，存在于自身不同时出现，但与相同的其他词位共同出现的词位之间。具有聚合关系的词位可以在同一上下文中相互替换。
        • DS reformulation
          • Words sharing syntagmatic contexts have similar paradigmatic properties.
          • 分布假说确实可以用结构主义术语重新表述：共享组合上下文的词具有相似的聚合属性
        • 帮助记忆
          • 核心观点：一个词的“价值（Valeur）”不是孤立存在的，而是在整个语言系统里通过与其他词的差异来定义的。他提出了两大关系：
            • 组合关系（Syntagmatic）：线性句子里的强行搭配（比如：“喝”后面接“水”）。
            • 聚合关系（Paradigmatic）：同一位置的无情替换（比如：“水”、“茶”、“可乐”都可以接在“喝”后面，它们互为替换关系）。
          • 通俗理解：索绪尔说，分布假说用我的话翻译就是：“共享相同‘组合环境’的词，必然具备相似的‘聚合替换属性’”
      • Corpus Linguistics (John R. Firth)
        • Famous maxim: "You shall know a word by the company it keeps!" (aligned with Wittgenstein's "meaning is use").
        • Collocations: Lexical items that habitually co-occur in text, displaying an "order of mutual expectancy."
        • Meaning is directly defined by these collocates.
        • 帮助记忆
          • 习惯搭配论
          • 核心观点：名言 “你要通过一个词的伴随者来认识它！”（You shall know a word by the company it keeps!）。他提出了搭配（Collocation）的概念，认为词与词之间有一种“相互期待”的秩序。
          • 通俗理解：Firth 更加接地气。他说，比如“dark（黑暗）”和“night（夜晚）”就是一对刻在骨子里的铁杆CP，它们习惯性地天天黏在一起。你懂了它们之间的强绑定和相互期待，你就懂了它们的词义。
      • Behaviorist Psychology (Miller & Charles)
        • Explains human word associations (e.g., stimulus dog →\rightarrow→​​​​​​​​​​​ response cat) via an associationist view of learning through co-occurrence
        • Contextual Hypothesis
          • Speakers form a mental contextual representation (knowledge of how a word is used) by repeatedly observing it
          • Two words are semantically similar to the extent that their mental contextual representations are similar
        • 帮助记忆
          • 心理认知论
          • 核心观点：人类是怎么学会新词的？就是靠高频观察它出现的上下文。大脑以此在内部建立了一个“Contextual Representation”。我们在心理上判断两个词像不像，其实就是在悄悄比对这两张心理认知图谱。
          • 通俗理解：前三位大哥都在研究冷冰冰的文本，心理学派则把这个规律搬进了人类的大脑。比如心理测验里我说“猫”，你脱口而出“狗”（词汇联想），这就是因为你大脑里的刺激信号经常共现，形成了一种条件反射式的认知表示
          • The analysis of word associations plays a central role in behaviorist psychology, which pursues an associationist view of meaning, based on the idea that simple association or co-occurrence of stimuli is the primary basis of thought and learning. 对词汇联想的分析在行为主义心理学中起着核心作用，该心理学追求一种关联主义的意义观，其基础是认为刺激的简单联想或共现是思想和学习的主要基础
          • Miller and Charles claim that speakers are able to acquire many new words only using distributional information. Miller 和 Charles 声称，说话者仅仅利用分布信息就能习得许多新词。
      • 对比
        • 相同点
          • 底层信念一致：四者都坚信“意义源于关联”，绝对反对孤立地去定义一个词。
          • 评判标准一致：都认同“上下文（邻居/环境）”是解开词义谜题的唯一钥匙。
        • 不同点
    • Modern Perspectives (Lenci, 2008)
      • Weak Distributional Hypothesis
        • DS is purely a computational tool/framework, providing a third type of empirical evidence alongside native speakers' intuitions and traditional dictionaries
          • 对词汇意义的调查通常依赖于两种证据：母语者对语言表达语义属性的直觉，以及词典等词汇资源中对意义的描述
          • 分布语义学增加了第三种经验证据：对语言上下文中词位分布的计算分析
      • Strong Distributional Hypothesis
        • DS is a cognitive model of semantic memory; word distributions in linguistic input directly shape human conceptual representations.
          • 分布语义学是一个构建语义记忆模型的理论和计算框架，其基础是假设语言输入中词汇的分布直接促成了其概念表示的决定
      • Pros of DS
        • Fast, cheap, unsupervised, data-driven, scales well with huge digital corpora, and models the mental lexicon efficiently
        • can rely on huge amounts of empirical evidence to characterize the semantic properties of lexemes
        • fast and cheap, and can be performed for any language or domain, as long as we have enough textual data
        • It is an efficient and effective method to build corpus-based lexical resources, and to learn semantic representations for NLP and AI systems
    • Computational Chronology
      • 1960s
        • Early attempts at building electronic thesauri (grouping words by paradigmatic relations) for machine translation; performance was unsatisfactory.
          • thesauri：同义词库 words are grouped together according to paradigmatic relations, like synonymy and hypernymy, or because they belong to the same semantic field
      • 1975
        • Introduction of the Vector Space Model (VSM) in information retrieval using a term-document matrix (rows = terms, columns = documents) to calculate similarity.
      • 1990
        • Breakthrough of Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA) 潜在语义分析
        • Applied Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to the term-document matrix to reduce dimensionality, filtering surface noise to uncover higher-order, latent semantic structures.
      • 2010s+
        • Rise of Deep Learning and Predict Models (neural language models)
        • Distributional vectors are popularized as word embeddings, which act as foundational features that can be directly used or fine-tuned for downstream AI applications
  • Section 2: Distributional Representations
    • The 5-Step Pipeline for Distributional Vectors
      • Extract target-context co-occurrences from a text corpus
      • Build a co-occurrence matrix (Rows = target lexemes, Columns = contexts, Entries = frequency weights)
      • Generate High-Dimensional Explicit Vectors directly from the matrix rows
      • Map (Optional) the matrix onto fewer latent dimensions to generate low-dimensional, dense word embeddings (Implicit Vectors) Implicit Vectors are also called Word Embeddings
        • Explicit vs. Implicit Distributional Vectors
          • Property
            Explicit Vectors (Co-occurrence Matrix Rows)
            Implicit Vectors (Word Embeddings)
            Dimension Meaning            		each vector dimension represents a (weighted) co-occurrence count with a specific contextk Latent features extracted via mathematical projection
            Dimensionality            		High-dimensional (scales with corpus vocabulary).Low-dimensional (typically a few hundred dimensions)
            Sparsity            		Sparse (mostly filled with zeros due to Zipf's law).Dense (mostly non-zero components)
            Interpretability            		High (each axis has a clear linguistic meaning)Low (dimensions represent abstract, unnamable semantic features)
            Relations Discovered            		First-order co-occurrences (lexical items that directly co-occur with the same contexts)High-order co-occurrences (or indirect co-occurrences) , lexemes co-occurring with contexts that are similar because they in turn share first-order co-occurrences
          • The Curse of Dimensionality
            • High-dimensional explicit spaces suffer from:
              • Distance Concentration
                • Vectors tend to become equidistant, making it impossible to separate similar items from dissimilar ones.
              • Hubness
                • "Hub" vectors emerge that falsely appear close to an unnaturally large number of other vectors.
              • memory consumption and computational complexity
                • The bigger the data, the more storage space the distributional vectors will require, and the operations we perform using the distributional vectors have a high computational cost
          • The Logic of Dimensionality Reduction (Feature Extraction)
            • Assumes raw corpus statistics are noisy
            • Projecting data onto a reduced space filters surface noise and captures high-order (indirect) co-occurrences (e.g., recognizing that buy and purchase are synonyms because their contexts share 1st-order co-occurrences
            • clustering them along an abstract latent axis such as "commercial transactions")
      • Measure semantic similarity between lexemes using vector similarity functions
        • 方便记忆
          • 从文本抽共现 ➡️ 画共现矩阵 ➡️ 提取矩阵的行作为高维显式向量 ➡️ (可选) 降维成低维隐式词嵌入 ➡️ 算向量相似度
          • extract co-occurrences from a text corpus, build the co occurrence matrix, use the matrix to generate high-dimensional explicit vectors, map the matrix onto fewer dimensions to get low-dimensional implicit vectors, calculate semantic similarity using vector similatiry
    • Distributional Vectors
      • The co-occurrence matrix represents lexemes as distributional vectors
      • Distributional vectors encode distributional features: the distributional vector of a target lexeme is a vector of distributional features representing its co-occurrence statistics. 分布向量编码了分布特征：目标词位的分布向量是一个代表其共现统计数据的分布特征向量
      • Co-occurrence matrices define distributional vector spaces and each row is the distributional vector of a target lexeme 共现矩阵定义了分布向量空间，每一行都是一个目标词位的分布向量
      • Therefore, they provide a geometrical representation of the lexicon as a semantic space, in which the position of any lexeme depends on its co-occurrences with linguistic contexts 因此，它们将词汇表几何化地表示为一个语义空间，其中任何词位的位置都取决于它与语言上下文的共现情况
    • Paradigm Shift in ML
      • Feature-Based Learning
        • In feature-based machine learning models, data is represented as manually selected feature vectors (during the feature engineering phase)
      • Representation Learning
        • Algorithms automatically transform raw data into numerical vectors during training, discovering the optimal features needed for a specific task without human intervention
        • The advantage of this approach is that it eliminates the need for manual feature selection, as the network automatically learns, during the training process, a representation of the data aspects that are most important for a given task
        • 方便记忆
          • 从过去需要人工抠特征的“特征工程”，升级为了“表示学习”（AI自动从数据中学习出对任务最有利的数值向量表示）
    • st.1 Corpus Challenges & Processing
      • Zipf's Law & Data Sparseness
        • Word distributions are heavily skewed
        • A tiny fraction of words are highly promiscuous, while the vast majority occur rarely
        • In the British National Corpus (BNC), 63% of co-occurrence pairs appear only once (hapax); only 7% appear ≥10\ge 10≥10​​​​​​ times.
      • Mitigation
        • Train on massive, multi-billion-word web corpora to smooth sparseness
        • enforce a frequency threshold (e.g., discard words appearing <50< 50<50​​​​ times)
        • and remove uninformative stop words (grammatical function words like the, and).
    • st.2 The Three Context-Framing Paradigms
      如何框定上下文
      • Window-Based Collocates
        • Context words within a specified linear distance (window) from the target
        • It is a bag-of-words model that ignores syntactic structure
          • Unit & Size
            • Measured in tokens
            • defined by left span mmm​ and right span nnn​
            • Total size k=m+n+1k = m + n + 1k=m+n+1​​​​​​​​​​​​​
            • Symmetric if m=nm = nm=n​​​​​, otherwise asymmetric.
          • Direction
            • Undirected (treats left and right uniformly) or Directed (distinguishes left/right context positions)
          • Shape
            • Rectangular (equal weight to all words in the window) or Triangular (closer words receive higher weights)
          • Pros/Cons
            • Extremely fast and simple to extract
            • completely blind to syntax.
      • Syntactic Collocates
        • Context words identified via structural dependency relations with the target
          • Syntactic co-occurrences are co-occurrences in which the context is a syntactic collocate of the target.
          • Parameters:
            • Relation type (e.g., subject, direct object)
            • Path length (usually restricted to length 1 for direct relations).
          • Pros/Cons:
            • Captures true linguistic structure beyond linear distance
            • highly complex to extract since it requires pre-parsed corpora.
      • Constructional Contexts
        • Utilizes complex, multi-word lexico-syntactic structures or abstract argument patterns (constructions) as the environment to differentiate verbs that share simple arguments
        • Motivation
          • The meaning of a lexeme is also strongly correlated with its syntactic environments
          • (e.g., distinguishing The doctor healed the patient vs. The poison killed the patient by treating the subject-object combination as a unified context)
          • 两个动词尽管在语义上差异很大，却可能拥有相同的独立搭配。在以下句子中，“杀死”和“治愈”虽然直接宾语相同，但如果将主宾组合视为一个整体语境，二者却截然不同
        • construction定义
          • 在语言学中，构式是指任何类型的、复杂的、具有特定含义的词汇-句法结构模板（Patterns of linguistic items） A construction is any kind of complex lexico-syntactic structure.
          • 构式有不同的“抽象级别”，就像程序员写代码的模板一样：
            • 完全具象的结构（Fully specified）：比如 “The player kicked the ball”。
            • 高度抽象的结构（Abstract structures / Argument patterns）：比如及物构式（Transitive Construction），它的模板是 [名词短语1] + [动词] + [名词短语2]；或者动词的子范畴化框架（Subcategorization frames）
          • 当模型使用“构式”作为上下文时，它看的是：“这个目标词，经常嵌入在哪些复杂的句法骨架（构式）当中？”
      • 对比
        • 窗口法（Window-based Collocates）—— “看线性邻居”
          • 做法：设定一个滑动窗口（比如左2右2），强行抓取周围的词。
          • 本质：词袋模型（Bag-of-Words）。
          • 特点：简单粗暴、速度极快，但完全无视语法和结构。
        • 句法搭配法（Syntactic Collocates）—— “看一阶亲戚”
          • 做法：利用依存句法分析，找出和目标词有直接语法关系的词（如直接的主谓、动宾，路径长度通常为1）。
          • 特点：考虑了语言结构；但依然是把主语、宾语拆开成独立的维度来统计。
        • 构式上下文（Constructional Contexts）—— “看整体骨架”
          • 做法：不把句法环境拆散，直接把整个复杂的 lexico-syntactic 复合结构或者论元模式（Argument patterns）作为矩阵里的一个“列标签（Column）”。
          • 特点：最为精确，能够完美区分像 healed 和 killed 这种在宏观结构中语义对立的词
    • st.2 Matrix Weighting Functions
      • Raw frequencies
        • Raw frequencies are suboptimal because high-frequency words dominate without providing semantic discrimination
        • Weighting co-occurrences with their raw frequency turns out not to be the optimal solution because of the skewed distribution of language data
        • with few very frequent lexical items and a large number of extremely rare ones
        • Frequent lexemes tend to co-occur with many different words
        • Such co-occurrences are uninformative, even if they are very frequent, since they lack semantic discriminative power
      • Advanced alternatives include
        • Binary Function
          • Entries are 1 if a co-occurrence exists, 0 otherwise
            完全无视频率的大小。只要目标词与这个上下文共同出现过，权重就直接填 1；如果没有出现过，就填 0
          • Flaw: Fails to distinguish casual, accidental co-occurrences from significant ones
            • 它无法区分“偶然共现”与“显著共现”。在海量语料库里，两个原本毫无关系的词可能因为打印错误或凑巧在一句话里出现了一次，二元函数就会给它打 1 分；而真正高频强相关的词也只有 1 分。因此，仅仅因为两个词共同出现过，并不足以说明这个上下文对目标词是有价值的
        • Logarithmic Transformation
          • Smoothes frequency distributions，giving less weight to high-frequency co-occurrences
        • Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI)
          • An association measure calculating how much the actual joint probability of a target (ttt​) and context (ccc​) exceeds their expected probability if they were completely independent 实际的共现概率，比假设这两者完全独立随机出现时的“期望”概率高出多少
            • PMI(t,c)=log⁡2P(t,c)P(t)×P(c)PMI(t, c) = \log_2 \frac{P(t, c)}{P(t) \times P(c)}PMI(t,c)=log2​P(t)×P(c)P(t,c)​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​
              • 大于0:正相关；等于0:不相关；小于0:负相关，排斥关系
                • 因为负值在距离计算中容易带来噪音，所以我们在工程上会使用 PPMI（Positive PMI），即：如果算出来是负数，就强行令其等于 0
              • 分子：目标词 ttt​ 和上下文 ccc​ 在语料库中真正同时出现（共现）的实际概率 分母：假设 ttt​ 和 ccc​ 这两个词在世界上完全独立、互不相干，它们仅仅因为各自的词频高，而在句子里随机撞在一起的“期望概率”
            • Implicit Context Selection
              • PMI automatically sets all negative association scores to 0, retaining only contexts with positive mutual information

  • Section 3: Distributional Semantic Models (DSMs)
    • A Distributional Semantic Model (DSM) is a computational model that learns distributional representations of target lexemes by encoding their co-occurrence statistics with linguistic contexts
    • Models are categorized by two major dimensions: context type and learning method.
      • 1. Classification by Context Type
        • Linguistic Models
          • Assume two lexemes are similar if they tend to co-occur with the same linguistic units (e.g., window-based collocates or syntactic constructions).
        • Document Models
          • Assume two lexemes are similar if they tend to co-occur within the same texts/documents.
      • 2. Classification by Learning Method
        • Count Models
          • Learn distributional vectors by directly recording and counting a target's co-occurrences with contexts.
        • Matrix Models (Subtype of Count Models)
          • Arrange global distributional statistics from a corpus into a co-occurrence matrix (can yield explicit vectors or embeddings via SVD reduction).
          • Three Drawbacks of Matrix Models:
            • Efficiency: Building and storing massive matrices requires enormous computational resources and memory as the corpus scales.
            • Scalability: Struggles to handle exponentially growing, massive datasets.
            • Incrementality: Matrix DSMs are batch learners. They lack incrementality because they rely on global statistics. If new text data is added, the entire semantic space must be rebuilt again from scratch.
        • Random Encoding Models
          • Avoid building global geometric matrices
          • Instead, they assign each lexeme a random vector and update it incrementally as new contexts are encountered, directly generating low-dimensional embeddings.
        • Predict Models
          • Do not count co-occurrences
          • Instead, they use neural networks trained as language models to predict the contexts of a target lexeme, generating embeddings incrementally as a by-product of this prediction task.
          • Static vs. Contextual Embeddings (The Paradigm Shift)
            • Static DSMs: Assign a single, stable, out-of-context distributional vector to each word type (e.g., word2vec). It cannot natively handle polysemy (words with multiple meanings).
            • Contextual DSMs (e.g., BERT): Assign a unique, context-sensitive token vector to each individual word instance based on its specific sentence environment. It computes vectors as a function of the internal activation states of a deep neural network, meaning the same word token yields distinct vectors in different sentences.

    • Fundamental Concepts & Architecture
      • Representation Learning
        • Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) automatically discover implicit representations and statistical patterns directly from raw data, bypassing manual feature engineering.
      • Predict Models
        • The embeddings generated by neural models are the by-product of training a network to predict neighboring words
      • Micro-mechanics of a Neuron
        • The basic processing unit
        • It receives inputs (xxx​), multiplies them by their connection weights (www​), sums them, and applies an activation function (ggg​) to produce an output (yyy​).
      • Network Layers
        • Hidden Layers
          • Layers situated between the input and output layers
          • Models with many hidden layers are called Deep Neural Networks (Deep Learning).
      • Feedforward Network / Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)
        • A network where computation flows strictly forward from input to output through hidden layers via fully connected layers (each neuron connects to all neurons in the next layer).
      • Perceptron
        • The simplest network architecture, containing no hidden layers.
      • Weight Matrices (WWW​)
        • Represent connections between layers mathematically
        • An MLP with one hidden layer contains two weight matrices: W∗1W*1W∗1​​​ (input-to-hidden) and W∗2W*2W∗2​​​ (hidden-to-output).
    • The Optimization and Training Cycle
      • Supervised / Error-Driven Learning
        • An iterative training process where the system compares its predicted output (yyy​) against the expected target output (ttt​), calculates the error, and adjusts its parameters to minimize it.
      • Self-Supervised Learning
        • Even though neural networks use supervised backpropagation, language modeling is considered unsupervised/self-supervised because it requires no human-annotated labels; the training target is derived directly from the raw input text sequence.
      • Random Initialization
        • Weight matrices must be initialized with random numbers at the start of training
        • This breaks network symmetry, preventing different neurons from computing identical values and allowing each weight to learn independently
      • Loss Function (Objective/Cost Function)
        • A function that projects the expected vector (ttt​) and predicted vector (yyy​) into a single real number representing a distance/loss score (quantifying the network's prediction error).
      • Optimizer & Backpropagation
        • The optimizer manages weight updates based on the gradient of the loss function
        • The backpropagation algorithm propagates the error signal backward through all layers, determining each weight's contribution to the error and indicating the direction and intensity (gradient) to modify them
      • Hyperparameters
        • Variables defined a priori (before training) to regulate the learning process (e.g., the number of layers, network dimensionality/neuron count).
        • Epoch
          • One complete training cycle where the neural network has processed the entire training dataset exactly once.
        • Batches
          • Subsets of the dataset provided to the network in iterations. Training in batches allows weights to be updated more frequently per epoch, improving generalization and reducing computational load.
      • Overfitting
        • A phenomenon where a model adapts too well to the training data, memorizing its specific noise and traits rather than learning recurring patterns
        • This prevents the model from generalizing to new, unseen data, causing its performance on external tasks to deteriorate.
      • Development / Validation Set
        • A dedicated portion of annotated data kept separate from the training set
        • It is used exclusively to evaluate the network's performance and generalization ability at regular intervals during training.
    • Key Neural Implementations
      • Word2vec (Mikolov et al., 2013)
        • The foundational predict model framework. It treats context as a bag-of-words, completely ignoring element order.
        • CBOW (Continuous Bag-Of-Words)
          • Predicts a single target word based on the surrounding context words.
        • Skip-Gram (SG)
          • Predicts the surrounding context words based on a single target word.
      • FastText
        • An extension of word2vec that learns embeddings for character n-grams instead of whole word types
        • A word's vector is the sum of its subword n-gram embeddings
        • This drastically improves the model's ability to handle out-of-vocabulary (OOV) and rare words
      • Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)
        • Introduced to preserve language's sequential order and temporal succession, which bag-of-words models ignore
        • RNNs process one element at a time, feeding the hidden layer's output from the previous step back into the current step via a context layer (multiplied by a context matrix)
        • This provides the network with a temporal "memory" of what it has already processed
  • Section 5: Evaluation of Distributional Semantic Models
    • Evaluation determines how good a model is or which model suits a specific application
    • Semantic Foundations: Two Types of Similarity
      • There is no absolute definition of similarity
      • Psychologists (Medin et al., 1993) distinguish between:
        • Attributional Similarity
          • Defined between lexemes that share semantic attributes or features (e.g., dog and cat are similar because both are small, domestic, four-legged animals).
        • Relational Similarity
          • Defined between pairs of lexemes that share a similar relation or function, even if they share zero physical attributes (e.g., leg and wheel are relationally similar because both act as components that enable ground movement for humans and cars, respectively).
    • The Two Evaluation Dimensions
      • Intrinsic Evaluation
        • Evaluates a computational model based on the quality of its direct, raw output (independent of external applications).
        • Intrinsic Evaluation Test Suite (5 Core Tests)
          • Synonym Tests (e.g., TOEFL)
            • Multiple-choice vocabulary tests where the model must pick the correct synonym for a target word out of several alternatives. It calculates the cosine similarity between vectors; the model is correct if the true synonym scores highest. Metric: Accuracy.
          • Similarity and Relatedness Tests (e.g., RG65)
            • Models score word pairs rated by humans (on scales like 0–4). The model's success is measured by the Spearman rank correlation (ρ\rhoρ​​​​) between the model's cosine scores and human rankings.
          • Categorization (Clustering) Tests
            • Evaluates if a DSM captures taxonomic similarity by feeding vectors into a clustering algorithm. The resulting groups are compared against gold-standard, semantically coherent reference classes.
          • Analogy Tests (a:b=c:da : b = c : da:b=c:d​​​​​​​​​​​​​)
            • Tests relational similarity (e.g., wheel : car = finger : hand).
            • Analogy Completion Task
              • Inferred as a:b=c:?a : b = c : ?a:b=c:?​​​​​​​​​​​​​ (e.g., Italy : Rome = Sweden : ? →\rightarrow→​​​​​​​​​​​ Stockholm).
          • Relation & Psycholinguistic Tests
            • Tests the ability to discriminate specific lexical relations (e.g., Hypernymy) or replicates human free association tasks (measuring if a model's nearest neighbors match the first words that pop into a human subject's head when prompted with a stimulus word).
      • Extrinsic Evaluation
        • Assesses a model's quality based on its impact on a downstream, external NLP application/task
        • Uses embeddings as features in supervised ML architectures
        • This is a prime example of Transfer Learning (pretraining embeddings unsupervised on large text data, then initializing a network with them to boost performance on a specific target task)
